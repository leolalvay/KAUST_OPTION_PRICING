📝 Summary
On 2026-01-12, the team presented and debated progress on efficient American option pricing back-end methods, contrasting a Laplace-based baseline with a multilevel functional regression framework that leverages Markovian projection and optimal transport. Over three months, the codebase was refactored and documented, bugs were fixed, and a comparison framework reproduced the Laplace paper’s results to ~95% accuracy without access to the original code. Stress tests, performance analyses, and revised cost metrics were conducted, including optimal sample allocation studies and memory profiling.
Methodologically, Raúl clarified the approach is not classical MLMC: it relies on functional approximation and projection rather than solely telescopic expectation decompositions with standard couplings. The core idea is to approximate quantities like PB or PB² as functions of a low-dimensional projection S = p⋅x via Markovian projection, breaking the curse of dimensionality and enabling PDE-based optimal control. A key challenge is coefficient dependence (notably the volatility term b) on full paths, limiting direct application of transport maps when coefficients are not functions of p⋅x alone. Slides will be updated to explicitly tie X and S and correct the transport minimization formulation.
Across levels, a central difficulty is that PB_L − PB_{L−1} is evaluated on different discretizations x_L vs x_{L−1}. To avoid duplicating dimensionality, the team proposed transport maps that align path distributions across levels so differences can be expressed in terms of a single low-dimensional variable. Raúl’s convergence intuition supports identity-plus-small-correction mappings as Δt → 0; after projection to 1D, the optimal transport is given by inverse CDFs, enabling explicit couplings without high-dimensional maps. Empirically, Gaussian Brenier-style couplings yielded strong variance reduction and stability versus standard random-number aggregation, though a precise variance definition and formal integration of optimal transport into the problem statement were requested.
Comparative experiments examined surface agreement (PB²), convergence and uncertainty, scaling and timing, PDE accuracy, and parameter sensitivity. Results showed near-identical surfaces under verification, orders-of-magnitude runtime improvements from multilevel telescoping (many cheap coarse steps, fewer fine steps), and matched PDE solutions with minor sign-changing error behavior. Memory profiling revealed a quadratic bottleneck from dense d×d covariance/Gramian structures in regression; mitigations include 1D transport maps, sparsity, or H-matrix representations. Parameter studies highlighted inflated relative errors at low volatility and short maturity due to small denominators; a blended absolute-relative error metric with a monetary threshold was recommended.
Stress tests in high dimensions (up to 15) demonstrated MLMC-style multilevel regression stability where Laplace often failed (non–positive-definite Hessians producing NaNs), attributed to Laplace’s pointwise nature versus MLMC’s globally defined regressions that safely extrapolate over time and basket values. Sample allocation for stable least-squares regression followed conservative quadratic scaling with respect to subspace dimension, grounded in Gramian condition numbers and large-deviation results; experiments with constants C=5, 80, 150 yielded similar volatility surfaces, suggesting robustness and potential over-allocation. The team emphasized verification (code/results vs. math) over validation (vs. empirical reality), clarified log-transform Gaussianization is model-dependent (valid under Black–Scholes), and discussed terminology updates to reflect “multilevel regression” rather than MLMC/MLNC.
Implementation notes include documenting coupling strategies (standard aggregation vs. Gaussian/Brenier maps), refactoring for reproducibility, preparing notebooks, and integrating formal mathematical derivations (from GitHub PDFs/markdowns) into slides. Stability diagnostics will report condition numbers of AᵀA; accuracy is governed by subspace choice once stability is ensured, with minimal runtime differences across regularizations observed so far. Future work targets a 1D transport demonstrator reducing memory without sacrificing accuracy or effort, potential GPU/JAX acceleration once methods are controlled, and preparing a manuscript. Scheduling and collaboration logistics were noted (Raúl’s travel window, post-Ramadan availability, VPN access), with plans for continued coordination and possible co-tutorials.

4. Coupling Strategies and Optimal Transport
Standard coupling: aggregate fine-level increments to produce coarse increments; heuristic and not provably optimal.
Gaussian/Brenier optimal transport: closed-form for Gaussian measures; improved variance reduction and stability observed.

5. Multilevel Decomposition and Path Alignment
PB²_L = PB²_0 + Σ (PB²_l − PB²_{l−1}); differences depend on x_l vs x_{l−1}, risking dimensional blow-up.
Use transport maps to align distributions so differences can be approximated as functions of a single low-dimensional variable (S), benefiting stability and regression.
6. Convergence Intuition and 1D Transport Maps
As Δt → 0, xl ≈ xl−1 plus small random corrections; projections preserve or reduce differences.
In 1D, optimal transport is inverse CDF; enables explicit mapping Pxl−1 → Pxl, rewriting multilevel least-squares in single-level variables compatible with PDE inputs.
